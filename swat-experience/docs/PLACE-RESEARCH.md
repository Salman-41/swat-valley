## Mahodand — A lake is part of a river

Mahodand sits in the upper Ushu landscape beyond Kalam. The provincial tourism guide places the lake beyond Matiltan; its position belongs to a tributary valley rather than the main north–south line of Swat’s towns. [KP Tourism guide]

At an elevation reported as 2,865 metres in a published lake study, water, meadow and high mountain slopes form a connected environment. The photograph can introduce that setting; the geographic atlas explains how this upper place relates to Kalam downstream. [Bilal et al.]

Remote scenery also has a material footprint. In samples collected in June and July 2019, researchers detected microplastics in surface water and in the digestive tracts of brown trout at Mahodand. Their results describe a particular study, not a live measure of water quality and not a health assessment for today’s visitors. [Bilal et al.]

Connections: Kalam → the confluence; Changing landscape → material traces beyond the photograph. Language copy may refer to the wider Gawri-speaking upper valley, but should not assign one identity to every person at the lake. Do not call introduced trout native, describe the lake as pristine, or provide unsourced fishing permissions.

Sources: https://old.kptourism.com/uploads/images/emagazine/1613314124030-swat.pdf ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9865100/ ; DOI https://doi.org/10.3390/toxics11010003 ; publisher PDF mirror used to verify section 2.1 and sampling dates: https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/139a/9ab764db03487203121bae5c1a2c08e0dbbd.pdf .

## Kalam — Where two rivers become one

Kalam stands at the meeting of the Ushu and Utrot rivers, the confluence from which the Swat River flows. The upper valley divides into distinct branches: Ushu to the northeast and Utrot to the west. [Baart & Sagar]

Gawri, also called Kalami, belongs to this lived geography. Research by linguist Joan Baart and community researcher Muhammad Zaman Sagar describes closely related speech around Kalam, Ushu and Utrot, alongside other language communities. [Baart & Sagar]

The mountain town connects landscapes and livelihoods. A 2020 provincial tourism project assessment identified pressure on forests, sewage management, solid waste and unplanned building as issues in Kalam. These are dated planning findings, not a current inspection. [KITE framework]

Connections: Mahodand → Ushu branch; Bahrain → downstream; Voices → language teaching and documentation. Do not apply the old language paper’s 1990s population, education or hotel counts to the present. Spellings Ushu/Usho and Utrot/Utror vary across sources; use Ushu and Utror in site labels, mention the variation once in Sources.

Sources: https://fli-online.org/documents/languages/gawri/gawri_introduction.pdf (sections 1.1–1.2); https://old.kptourism.com/uploads/images/downloads/1586272079279KITE%20E%26S%20Management%20Framework%2C%20updated%20with%20COVID-19%20checklists.pdf (section 4.4.2); community materials: https://gawri.org/en/node/6 .

## Bahrain — The river carries a language

Between Madyan and Kalam, Bahrain is a riverside town where the road, bazaar and water occupy a narrow shared landscape. The provincial tourism guide also draws attention to the town’s wooden architectural details. [KP Tourism guide]

Bahrain is an important centre of Torwali language life. In his Smithsonian essay, educator and cultural organiser Zubair Torwali describes multilingual everyday life and the work of sustaining music, poetry and language. [Torwali, 2024]

His account follows the making of the Manjoora song collection and the Simam Festival. These are named, documented community initiatives: language renewed through performance and use, rather than an anonymous story invented for visitors. [Torwali, 2024]

Connections: Voices → read the author’s own account; Madyan and Kalam → adjacent river towns. Link to documented recordings rather than adding invented ambient folk music. A community account is a named perspective, not a complete ethnography. Do not turn tentative ancient ancestry narratives into settled archaeological claims.

Sources: https://old.kptourism.com/uploads/images/emagazine/1613314124030-swat.pdf ; https://folklife.si.edu/magazine/stream-of-voices-torwali-language-music-poetry-pakistan ; community organisation: https://torwali.org/index.php/en/revitalization-endangered-music%E2%80%94-case-torwali-music .

## Madyan — Knowledge in the side valleys

Madyan lies on the Swat River corridor south of Bahrain. It connects the main valley to neighbouring settlements and mountain valleys; its position makes a useful pause between the urban lower valley and Kalam’s upper branches. [GeoNames/OSM; KITE road sequence]

The landscape also holds detailed local knowledge. A 2023 ethnobotanical study by researchers including University of Swat scholars documented plant knowledge in Madyan and seven neighbouring villages, using interviews and fieldwork. [Dilbar et al.]

Such research changes what a landscape can mean: slopes are also places known through plant names, gathering and use. The study records reported practices; it does not establish that a plant is safe or clinically effective, and those practices should not become visitor instructions. [Dilbar et al.]

Connections: Bahrain → upstream; Mingora → lower valley; Voices → names and knowledge. Avoid assigning Madyan an exclusive language identity. An older linguistic survey describes Pashto predominance up to Madyan and Torwali further north, but these are historical survey observations rather than a current border. Do not publish medicinal preparation instructions.

Sources: https://mapcarta.com/14707502 ; https://old.kptourism.com/uploads/images/downloads/1586272079279KITE%20E%26S%20Management%20Framework%2C%20updated%20with%20COVID-19%20checklists.pdf (section 4.5.1); Dilbar et al., *Ethnobotany Research and Applications* 26:26 (2023), https://doi.org/10.32859/era.26.26.1-15 ; journal record https://ethnobotanyjournal.org/index.php/era/issue/view/65 .

## Mingora — The present beside the past

Mingora is Swat’s principal market town, part of the populated lower valley rather than an isolated mountain retreat. Nearby Saidu Sharif and the archaeological places around it make contemporary urban life and older material histories visible in the same area. [Baart & Sagar; DOAM]

At Butkara I, the surviving remains include a central stupa with smaller votive stupas, viharas and columns. The Department of Archaeology and Museums records schist paving and a water tank as part of the complex. [DOAM Butkara I]

Many sculptures found during excavation are now held in Swat Museum. That relationship between excavated site and museum collection offers two ways of reading a place: the layout on the ground, and objects studied and conserved elsewhere. [DOAM Butkara I]

Connections: Heritage → Butkara and Barikot; Barikot → another lower-valley urban landscape; atlas → the distance between urban centre and upper valley. Keep **Mingora** and **Saidu Sharif** distinct. Do not imply Butkara’s photograph depicts Mingora’s present city centre. DOAM’s broad “1st–5th century CE” catalogue period is not a definitive founding date for every phase at Butkara; avoid a single unsupported foundation date.

Sources: https://fli-online.org/documents/languages/gawri/gawri_introduction.pdf ; https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/3304 . Bonus accurate Butkara site marker: [72.36793, 34.76573], from DOAM. Do not confuse the separately catalogued Butkara rock art, https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/9896, with the stupa complex.

## Barikot — A town in layers

Barikot’s archaeological landscape joins a hilltop acropolis to the ancient lower town beside the Swat River. Excavators study the relationship between the elevated stronghold and the settlement on the plain. [Khyber PATH]

The team’s current chronology describes ten cultural macro-phases from around 1200 BCE to the premodern period. In later phases, defensive structures, housing and sacred buildings were changed and reused. The remains show a town repeatedly remade. [Khyber PATH]

A 2021–2022 excavation report records an apsidal Buddhist sacred building, a small cemetery and a secondary city gate. These are different parts of urban life, not evidence that the whole settlement had only one purpose. [Olivieri et al.]

Bazira–Barikot appears on Pakistan’s UNESCO Tentative List. This identifies a property proposed by the state for possible future nomination; it is not an inscription on the World Heritage List. [UNESCO]

Connections: Heritage → chronology and evidence; Mingora → Butkara and museum; Changing landscape → protecting material remains within a living valley. Prefer archaeology’s measured sequence over superlatives such as “oldest Buddhist temple in the world”. UNESCO’s submitted nomination text and the excavation team use different chronological summaries; use “occupation across many periods” in short copy rather than force a single origin date.

Sources: https://pric.unive.it/projects/khyberpath/research-and-excavations/site-1-barikot ; https://iris.unive.it/handle/10278/5017041 ; https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/7034/ .

## Malam Jabba — Beyond the main current

Malam Jabba lies east of Mingora, away from the chain of towns beside the main Swat River. Its geographic place in this experience is a side journey into mountain slopes, not another point along an invented river line. [Gazetteer position; geographic inference]

The hill station is associated with skiing and seasonal outdoor recreation. The resort’s own website distinguishes summer and winter activities; these are facilities in a lived mountain landscape, not the whole identity of the place. [Resort operator]

Forest research adds another scale of attention. A 2015 survey sampled thirteen forest stands around Malam Jabba and identified communities including blue pine, Himalayan spruce and fir. The researchers recorded disturbed size structures and gaps in regeneration in the sampled stands. [Siddiqui et al.]

Connections: Changing landscape → how forests renew; Mingora → geographical relationship; atlas → eastern branch. The forest study is a dated sample, not a current survey of the entire district. Do not claim every slope has the same forest, guarantee snow, repeat an unverified resort elevation, or invent ancient monastery counts around the resort.

Sources: https://mapcarta.com/15310258 ; https://malamjabbaresort.com/ ; https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/PDFs/47(6)/14.pdf .

## Shared environmental context (keep separate from travel advice)

A peer-reviewed 2025 study combines field investigation, remote sensing and modelling to examine the 2022 Swat floods and debris flows. It connects extreme rainfall, slope conditions, land cover and settlement exposure. Use its event-specific findings to explain a catchment; do not present simulated depths as universal measurements or future flood forecasts. Source: https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/25/1071/2025/index.html , DOI https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-25-1071-2025 .
